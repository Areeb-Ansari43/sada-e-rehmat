Be cautious with urgency

Be careful if someone says you must pay immediately, refuses to provide written terms, or cannot clearly explain what the price includes. Genuine availability can change, but pressure is not a substitute for clear documentation.

Keep a complete payment trail

Use a traceable payment method where possible, keep receipts, save the quotation, and record the name of the business and the person you spoke with. Do not send money to an unrelated personal account without understanding the relationship and receiving proper documentation.

Protect your personal documents

Passport and identity information can be sensitive. Confirm who is requesting it, why it is needed, how it will be stored, and how to contact the organisation later. Avoid sending documents through random social-media accounts.

Stay connected as a group

Agree meeting points, save the hotel address, keep a charged phone, and share an emergency contact with your companions. Older travellers and children should know what to do if separated. Your guide should be able to explain the group’s support arrangements clearly.

If something feels wrong

Pause, ask for written clarification, and speak to a trusted family member or adviser before paying. If you believe you have been targeted by fraud, keep all evidence and report it through the appropriate local authority. UK travellers can consult current FCDO and Action Fraud guidance.
