What to pack for Hajj or Umrah without overpacking

The best packing list is not the longest one. It is the list that helps you walk comfortably, stay organised, manage the heat, and keep essential documents and medication close at hand.

Documents and daily essentials

Pack your passport, visa or booking documents, insurance details, emergency contacts, accommodation information, and copies stored separately. A small cross-body or waist bag can help you keep essentials close while leaving your hands free.

Clothing and footwear

Choose modest, breathable clothing suitable for warm weather and long walks. Break in footwear before the trip and bring a second comfortable option if space allows. Pack layers for air-conditioned transport and cooler evenings, especially if you are travelling from the UK or northern Pakistan.

Health and personal care

Bring prescribed medication in its original packaging, a simple list of medicines and doses, any necessary medical letters, basic toiletries, sun protection, and rehydration supplies where permitted. Check current Saudi rules before bringing medicines, particularly controlled or prescription medicines.

What belongs in your day bag

  • Phone and power bank.
  • Water bottle where permitted.
  • Small tissues and hand hygiene supplies.
  • Medication needed during the day.
  • Hotel card or address.
  • Light layer and sun protection.

What not to bring

Avoid filling your case with items you can buy easily or will not use. Heavy luggage creates extra work when moving between airports, hotels, and buses. Leave space for any permitted items you may bring home, and label your bags clearly.

Important: Requirements, visa routes, health rules, prices, and official booking arrangements can change. Check current official guidance for your nationality and dates before you travel.
