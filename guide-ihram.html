Guides · Rules of Ihram

Ihram rules, made simple.

Ihram is both what you wear and the sacred state you enter with your intention. Once you say the Talbiyah, a few everyday things become forbidden until your Umrah or Hajj is complete.

In short

  • Ihram begins with the intention and Talbiyah, not with putting on the clothes.
  • Men: two unstitched white sheets, head uncovered, sandals that leave the ankles bare.
  • Women: ordinary modest clothing of any colour; face and hands uncovered (no niqab or gloves).
  • Forbidden for all: perfume, cutting hair or nails, intimacy, hunting, arguing.

What Ihram is

Ihram is the state a pilgrim enters before Umrah or Hajj. It starts with a sincere intention (niyyah) and the Talbiyah, made before crossing the Miqat. The white sheets men wear are also called Ihram, but wearing them without the intention does not put you in Ihram, so you can change at home and make the intention later on the plane.

It is sunnah to bathe beforehand, trim nails and remove unwanted hair, and for men to apply fragrance to the body (not the clothing). Many pilgrims pray two rak'ahs before making the intention if it is not a disliked time.

What men and women wear

Men

  • Two seamless white sheets: the izar around the waist, the rida' over the shoulders
  • Head and face uncovered
  • Sandals or flip-flops that leave the ankles and top of the foot bare
  • No underwear, socks or stitched, fitted clothes

Women

  • Any loose, modest clothing, in any colour (white is common, not required)
  • Hijab as usual
  • Face and hands uncovered: no niqab or gloves
  • Normal shoes and socks are fine

Forbidden and allowed in Ihram

Not allowed

  • Perfume or scented products on body or clothes
  • Cutting or removing hair or nails
  • Marital relations, intimacy, or a marriage contract
  • Hunting land animals
  • Men: covering the head, fitted clothing
  • Women: niqab and gloves
  • Arguing, swearing or obscene talk

Allowed

  • Showering, with unscented soap and shampoo
  • A belt or money pouch around the waist
  • Watch, glasses, ring, hearing aid
  • Umbrella or shade (just not a cap on the head for men)
  • Changing into a clean set of Ihram
  • Medicines, plasters and unscented creams
  • Washing and scratching gently, even if a hair falls by accident

If you make a mistake

Anything done through genuine forgetfulness or ignorance is generally forgiven; stop as soon as you remember. Deliberately doing a forbidden act usually requires a compensation (fidyah): fasting three days, feeding six poor people, or sacrificing a sheep. Intimacy before the end of the pillars is the most serious breach.

Ask before you act

Penalties depend on the act and the school of law you follow. Ask your group scholar rather than guessing, especially for anything involving hair, intimacy or leaving the Miqat without Ihram.

Ihram packing list

  • Two sets of Ihram (men), so one can dry
  • Ihram belt with pockets
  • Unscented soap, shampoo, deodorant and wet wipes
  • Vaseline or anti-chafe balm for the thighs
  • Comfortable sandals, already worn in
  • Safety pins (to fasten, not to stitch)

Questions

Can I wear a belt in Ihram?

Yes. Men can wear a belt or money pouch to hold the izar and keep valuables, and women can wear whatever they normally wear.

Can I use soap while in Ihram?

Yes, as long as it is unscented. The same applies to shampoo, deodorant and moisturiser.

Can men wear underwear under Ihram?

No. Men should not wear any stitched, fitted garment, including underwear and vests. An anti-chafe balm helps.

Can women wear any colour?

Yes. Women's Ihram is ordinary modest clothing and does not have to be white.
